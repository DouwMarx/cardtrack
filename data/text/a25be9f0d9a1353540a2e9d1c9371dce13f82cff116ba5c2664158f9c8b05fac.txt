Kimi K3.1 Pro, the latest model from Moonshot AI, is 2nd among open-weight models on our Bio Capabilities Index (BCI), and 19th overall. Its score of 149 places it just behind Kimi K3 (150) and well behind the highest-scoring model, Claude Opus 5 (177). With respect to safeguards, K3.1 Pro refuses only 33.6% of hazardous prompts in our BioTIER benchmark.
Kimi K3.1 Pro was released via Moonshot’s API on October 23, 2026, and Moonshot plans to publish its weights by November 4. This analysis includes results from our knowledge benchmarks and safeguard evaluations; agentic benchmark scores will follow on our dashboard.
Kimi K3.1 Pro’s biology capabilities are similar to Kimi K3’s, about a year behind the closed-weight frontier
On SecureBio's Bio Capabilities Index (BCI), which aggregates multiple biology benchmarks into a single comparable number, Kimi K3.1 Pro scores 149. That places it 2nd of the 36 open-weight models we’ve evaluated, just behind Kimi K3 (150), which was released in July, and tied with Alibaba’s Qwen3.8 (149). Among all evaluated models, K3.1 Pro is 19th, still substantially behind leading closed-weight models. The current top open-weight score was first passed by Google’s Gemini 3 Pro (156) in November 2025, about 11 months before K3.1 Pro’s release.

Kimi K3.1 Pro rarely refuses to answer hazardous biological questions
Our BioTIER benchmark measures whether models refuse hazardous biological queries and permit benign ones. K3.1 Pro refuses 33.6% of the questions in our BioTIER-refuse set and correctly permits 99.9% of our BioTIER-permit set. For hazardous refusals, this is a modest improvement from 26.9% for Kimi K3, but still far behind the classifiers and refusals of closed-weight providers. We test K3.1 Pro through Moonshot’s API, so our BioTIER numbers include production safeguards. Once the model weights are released, safeguards can likely be further removed by fine-tuning.

Analysis of Moonshot’s safety information
Moonshot has not published a system card or safety report for Kimi K3.1 Pro, and its launch post does not discuss biological risk. Moonshot does conduct data filtering for cybersecurity, which it finds leads to a meaningful reduction in cyber capabilities, intentionally below the capabilities of frontier closed-weight models. Moonshot states that it aims “not to release open weights that would extend the absolute frontier of cyber capability.” This demonstrates that open-weight model providers can take steps towards withholding dual-use capabilities in released models.
Additional benchmark findings
Our knowledge benchmarks cover a wide range of biology and biosecurity-relevant topics. Kimi K3.1 Pro achieves the top open-weights score on two (VCT-v2 and MBCT) and improves on Kimi K3 on three of the four we highlight.
- Virology Capabilities Test (VCT-v2): 46.2 ± 1.2% (0.0% refused). K3.1 Pro places 8th overall on VCT-v2, up from 41.8% for Kimi K3 and the highest open-weight score, although far behind the frontier represented by GPT-6 Astra (58.0%).
- Molecular Biology Capabilities Test (MBCT): 60.7 ± 0.9% (0.0% refused). K3.1 Pro ranks 5th overall, about 3 points behind GPT-6 Astra (63.9%). It has the highest open-weight score, ahead of Kimi K3 (58.4%).
- World-Class Biology (WCB): 51.5 ± 1.6% (0.9% refused). K3.1 Pro ranks 27th, below Kimi K3 (55.0%).
- Human Pathogen Capabilities Test (HPCT): 63.5 ± 1.2% (0.0% refused). K3.1 Pro ranks 8th overall and 2nd among open-weight models, behind GLM 5.2 (65.3%).

Implications for open-weight biosecurity
Kimi K3.1 Pro does not move K3’s open-weight frontier forward on our BCI, remaining about a year behind the closed-weight frontier to date. Moonshot’s biological safeguards remain weak, with only a third of hazardous prompts being refused through the API. For an open-weight model, it is possible to remove model-level refusal and API-level classifiers, but these safeguards still increase barriers to accessing hazardous biological information.
Moonshot’s decision to withhold cyber capabilities for its open-weight release demonstrates that open-weight model developers can make good-faith attempts to limit specific hazardous capabilities.
We will continue to evaluate new models as they are released and expect to have results for K3.1 Pro on our agentic benchmarks in the coming weeks.
