1       Introduction

GPT-6 Cyber is a cyber-specialized variant of GPT-6 Astra designed for authorized security
work, including tasks such as vulnerability discovery, exploit development, and web pentesting.
The model has specialized post-training that aims to improve cybersecurity capabilities and
reduce cyber refusals. The goal is to expand the security work the model can perform while
keeping it within the user’s authorized scope and preserving refusals outside cybersecurity. In our
evaluations, GPT-6 Cyber shows substantially stronger cybersecurity capabilities and improved
alignment compared with GPT-5.6 Cyber.

Under our Preparedness Framework, we give GPT-6 Cyber the same capability designations as
GPT-6 Astra: Critical in Cybersecurity, High in the Biological and Chemical domain, and below
High in AI Self-Improvement.

Access to GPT-6 Cyber is restricted through Daybreak Red, with identity verification, use
restrictions, and organizational security requirements including single sign-on, multi-factor au-
thentication, and API IP allowlisting. API customers using Zero Data Retention generally require
Private Safety Processing for automated safety review. New safeguards are designed to block
high-risk activity against critical systems and assistance enabling high risk activities, alongside
GPT-6 Astra’s monitoring for harmful behavior outside the user’s intent or authorization. See
Safeguards for details.

2       Model Data and Training

GPT-6 Cyber’s post-training focuses on cybersecurity capabilities and alignment. We use
reinforcement learning to train the model on tasks such as reproducing crashes, developing
working exploits, and searching codebases for vulnerabilities. Through this training, the model
learns to refine its approach, try different strategies, and recognize mistakes. We also use reward
signals designed to reduce refusals on authorized cybersecurity requests.

Alongside these capability improvements, we incorporate training signals to improve alignment
and discourage reward hacking. These signals reinforce following the user’s intended scope and
authorization, avoiding unrequested actions, and accurately reporting results. 1

3       Model Safety

GPT-6 Cyber’s training aims to reduce refusals on authorized cybersecurity tasks while maintaining
safety performance in other domains. We evaluate both objectives through tests of cyber refusals,
safe completion, and biological safety, alongside agentic evaluations of whether the model respects
the user’s intent and authorization.

4     Alignment and Agent Safety

GPT-6 Astra introduced substantial alignment improvements over GPT-5.6 Sol, as described in
its system card. Our goal with GPT-6 Cyber is to deliver similar alignment improvements while
enabling a broader range of authorized cybersecurity work. We use the same alignment evaluations
to test whether the model respects restrictions, reports obstacles honestly, and stays within the
user’s authorization. We report GPT-5.6 Sol and/or GPT-5.6 Cyber as the earlier-generation
baseline below.

GPT-6 Cyber shows substantial improvements over GPT-5.6 Sol. Its alignment results are broadly
comparable to GPT-6 Astra’s, with strengths and weaknesses across individual evaluations: it
matches Astra on respecting Auto-Review rejections and performs better at acknowledging
unavailable search tools, but is slightly less likely to stop when warnings warrant it and performs
worse at reporting blockers in its final answer.

4.4    Alignment on Previous Cases of Harmful Outcomes

We evaluated GPT-6 Cyber on difficult cases drawn from production conversations where Auto-
Review had blocked an action and further review identified an assistant failure. Each replay
begins shortly before the original failure, using simulated tool responses. The evaluation tests
whether the model repeats behaviors such as disclosing data, circumventing restrictions, or taking
destructive actions. GPT-6-Cyber’s flagged-action rate was 28.40%, close to GPT-6 Astra’s
26.70% and less than half GPT-5.6 Sol’s 64.53%. Because the evaluation deliberately selects
difficult cases involving prior failures, these rates do not represent the frequency of such behavior
in ordinary deployment.

5     Safeguards

                                                 8
We have built and deployed safeguards around GPT-6 Cyber to reinforce its safety and alignment
training. We designed these safeguards to keep the model’s actions within the user’s intent and
authorization and to block malicious use.

For GPT-6 Cyber, we have strengthened existing safeguards and introduced new protections
against high-risk cyber activity and unauthorized model actions.

5.1   Access to GPT-6 Cyber

Daybreak Red provides cyber-specialized models under stricter eligibility and security require-
ments. Like past models, GPT-6 Cyber is available only to approved Daybreak Red users and
organizations. We verify applicants’ identity and eligibility, require applicants to agree to restric-
tions on authorized activity and third-party access, and review eligibility based on organizations’
cybersecurity use cases, security practices, and legal attestations.

5.2   Existing safeguards

We first restrict access to approved users and have safeguards in place to protect their accounts
against takeover. For organizations, we require SSO, MFA, managed devices, and least-privilege
access, relying on customer attestation for MFA managed through their identity provider. Orga-
nizations are also required to protect and rotate credentials, monitor employee use, and report
suspected misuse or account compromise.

For individuals, we limit access to invited applicants who complete identity and liveness checks,
demonstrate professional suitability, and provide legal attestation. To protect their accounts, we
require Advanced Account Security and at least one physical FIDO2 security key.

Next, to help keep the model within authorized limits and prevent harmful actions, we recommend
using Codex’s sandboxing and Auto-Review features when accessing GPT-6 Cyber through the
Codex app. The sandbox limits the agent’s file and network access, while Auto-Review checks
eligible requests to act beyond those limits before execution. For GPT-6 Cyber API users, we
recommend adding comparable checks to the agent before sensitive actions and stopping execution
if review fails or denies an action.

Alongside these action checks, we recommend our approved users run security workflows in
isolated environments with explicit limits on targets, credentials, network access, and permitted
actions. For high-risk workflows, human operators should oversee the agent to be able to stop it.

Finally, we also use the same monitoring stack as GPT-6 Astra to detect potentially harmful
behavior outside the user’s intent or authorization. We will pause Codex rollouts for user
confirmation or terminate requests when monitoring identifies severe concerns. If an investigation
identifies misuse, we may restrict, suspend, or revoke access.

5.3   New and strengthened safeguards

GPT-6 Cyber’s stronger cybersecurity capabilities create greater risks of harm, so we have added
a series of new safeguards around the model.

Blocking high-risk cyber assistance. We added additional checks to GPT-6 Cyber and cyber-

critical models in Daybreak Blue to detect and block high-risk assistance involving industrial
controls, essential services, and election systems. These checks also target assistance that
could enable death or serious injury, catastrophic physical destruction, life-endangering service
disruption, or offensive control of weapons systems.

We evaluated these protections with Dragos, specialists in industrial control security, using
cyber-permissive Daybreak Red models in a controlled lab. Together, we developed a sensitive
red-teaming evaluation covering requests that progress from understanding a device to interacting
with it in dangerous or unauthorized ways. Below, we depict block rates on this evaluation across
our different trusted access tiers before and after these changes, including recall improvements
across our Standard access, Daybreak Blue, and Daybreak Red tiers.

                                              Figure 2

Figure: Safeguard block rate before and after updates across four access configurations on the

We continue to refine these safeguards using feedback from the security community, including
partner reports and reports submitted through /feedback in our first-party products when
safeguards miss harmful activity or interfere with legitimate work.

Cyber-specific misalignment monitoring. We adapted our monitoring instructions to
distinguish authorized security testing from actions outside the user’s scope. These instructions
help monitors assess whether the model’s use of systems, credentials, and data matches the user’s
authorization, without treating legitimate exploit development or testing as inherently misaligned.

Biological safety mode for all users. We also guard against the risk that cyber-focused
training could reduce refusals outside cybersecurity. We enable conservative biological safety mode
for every GPT-6 Cyber user, alongside biological safety classifiers and monitoring. Expanded
cyber access is not intended to relax our safeguards for other types of harm.

Private Safety Processing. Next, we’ve built automated safety checks that review related
interactions together to detect misuse that may not be apparent in a single request. To enable this,

                                                 10
we generally require API customers using Zero Data Retention to use Private Safety Processing
(PSP), with limited approved exceptions. Standard data retention remains the default for GPT-6
Cyber.

We use PSP to review encrypted safety records in a protected environment and produce limited
safety signals, without exposing the underlying prompts and responses to OpenAI personnel.
Customers retain the encrypted records in their own storage for 30 days and can investigate them
in their own systems or choose to share relevant information with us.

API IP allowlisting. To make stolen credentials harder to use, we require IP allowlisting
for Daybreak Red API access by default. This limits requests to customer-approved network
addresses. We approve exceptions for technical constraints; cloud-provider deployments may have
different requirements or approved alternatives.

Account-compromise monitoring. Finally, we monitor API keys and Sign in with ChatGPT
access tokens for signs of compromise across both Daybreak Red and Blue. When we detect
suspicious activity, we can investigate and revoke affected credentials.

6    Preparedness Evaluations

We evaluated GPT-6 Cyber using preparedness evaluations described in the GPT-6 Astra system
card, covering cybersecurity, biology and chemistry, and AI self-improvement. Because the
additional training focused on cybersecurity, we concentrated our testing and analysis there. We
expect capabilities outside cybersecurity to remain broadly similar to GPT-6 Astra’s.

Based on these evaluations, we give GPT-6 Cyber the same preparedness capability designations
as GPT-6 Astra: Critical in Cybersecurity, High in Biological and Chemical capabilities, and
below High in AI Self-Improvement.

7    Cybersecurity evaluations

Like GPT-6 Astra, we classify GPT-6 Cyber as Critical in Cybersecurity under our Preparedness
Framework. We report results at the highest tested reasoning budget using the evaluations
described in the Cybersecurity Capabilities section of the GPT-6 Astra system card.

We also worked with NIST CAISSI and Irregular on external assessments of GPT-6 Cyber’s
cybersecurity capabilities. Irregular found performance broadly comparable to GPT-6 Astra’s,
with higher success on long-horizon cyber tasks.

8     External Evaluations for Cyber

We collaborated with NIST's Center for Advancing Innovation and Standards for Super Intelligence
(NIST CAISSI) on measurement of cyber capabilities for GPT-6 Cyber. Separately, Irregular
evaluated GPT-6 Cyber’s cybersecurity capabilities, with its findings summarized below.

8.1   External Evaluations for Cyber Capabilities - Irregular

Irregular evaluated GPT-6 Cyber using the three cybersecurity evaluation suites described in
the GPT-6 Astra system card: CyScenarioBench, FrontierCyber, and Atomic Challenges. These
benchmarks have been updated since the GPT-6 Astra system card. We report both models’
results on the updated benchmarks, so Astra’s scores differ from those previously published.

On CyScenarioBench, which tests long-horizon offensive tasks, GPT-6 Cyber solved 11/11
challenges at least once, compared with 8/11 for GPT-6 Astra. Its average success rate was 61%,
an increase of 7 percentage points.

                                            Figure 9

On FrontierCyber, which tests vulnerability discovery and exploitation in real software and
hardware, GPT-6 Cyber solved 90/288 challenges, compared with 96/288 for GPT-6 Astra.
Successful attacks included zero-day vulnerabilities affecting browsers, mobile phones, and cloud
databases. However, Irregular observed no successful attacks on fully hardened targets, and
neither model solved any of the 15 highest-difficulty challenges.

10    Biological and chemical safety

We treat GPT-6 Cyber as High but not Critical in Biological and Chemical capabilities, consis-
tent with GPT-6 Astra’s Preparedness assessment. Because its additional training focused on
cybersecurity, we expected its biological and chemical capabilities to remain broadly similar to
Astra’s.

To test this expectation, we ran five Bio-High evaluations. Consistent with previous system cards,
we counted refusals as successes to estimate a conservative upper bound on capability. GPT-6
Cyber sometimes achieves higher raw scores, but they are largely due to fewer refusals; after
accounting for refusals and safe completions, its scores are comparable to GPT-6 Astra’s. On
ProtocolQA, GPT-6 Cyber scored 47.2% with standard scoring and 50.9% when we counted
refusals and safe completions as correct; both scores were higher than Astra’s corresponding
scores (41.4% and 45.4%), but both remained below ProtocolQA’s indicative High threshold of
54%. These results support carrying forward Astra’s assessment.
