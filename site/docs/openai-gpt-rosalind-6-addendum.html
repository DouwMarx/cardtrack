1 Introduction

GPT-Rosalind-6 is a frontier reasoning model for biology, drug discovery, and translational
medicine. We offer it as GPT-Rosalind-6 Research to qualified scientists, research institutes,
companies and government partners. Rosalind has fewer refusals on dual-use biological queries but
is only made available via trusted access. We review applicants’ proposed uses, security controls
and governance practices before granting access. We also continue to have other deployment
safeguards in place.

GPT-Rosalind-6 uses the same weights as GPT-6 Astra, our flagship reasoning model. It is
designed to support legitimate biological research, including some dual-use topics, while still
refusing requests for biological weaponization and retaining refusals on other safety topics.

Under our Preparedness Framework, we are treating this launch as High capability in the Biological
and Chemical domain, below the threshold for Critical. We rely on GPT-6 Astra’s capability
assessments for other Preparedness risks and treat GPT-Rosalind-6 as Critical in Cybersecurity
and below High in AI Self-Improvement.

This addendum describes GPT-Rosalind-6’s biological capability assessment, additional safety
and alignment evaluations, and deployment safeguards. We include results for GPT-Rosalind-5.5
where available for comparison. This addendum should be read alongside the GPT-6 Astra system
card, which describes the parent model’s broader capability and safety evaluations.

2 Model Data and Training

GPT-Rosalind-6 uses the same weights as GPT-6 Astra. We did not add training data or perform
additional post-training for this release. Details of the underlying model’s data and training are
provided in the GPT-6 Astra system card.1

3 Beneficial Capability Evaluations

We evaluated GPT-Rosalind-6 on three life sciences research benchmarks and compared its results
with GPT-Rosalind-5.5. The GPT-Rosalind-5.5 scores below use the same benchmark versions
and problem sets as GPT-Rosalind-6 and therefore differ from those reported at its launch. The
Preparedness evaluations in Section 4 assess capabilities relevant to the risk of severe harm. We
averaged response scores within each problem and then weighted the problems equally.

3.1 Medicinal chemistry

MedChemBench v7 tests chemical structure interpretation, structure-activity relationships, and
predictions of drug potency and toxicity. We evaluated both models on the same 99 problems, with
up to nine scored responses per problem at each reasoning budget. At the highest reasoning budget
tested, mean rubric scores were 49.6% for GPT-Rosalind-6 and 28.9% for GPT-Rosalind-5.5.

3.2 Genomics and quantitative biology

GeneBench Pro v13 tests an agent’s ability to plan and carry out quantitative analyses of
biological data. We estimated single-attempt success from seven responses per model on each of
129 problems at each reasoning budget. At the highest reasoning budget tested, single-attempt
success rates were 37.7% for GPT-Rosalind-6 and 16.7% for GPT-Rosalind-5.5. The evaluation
used a benchmark-specific configuration that differs from production.

3.3 Applied life sciences research

LifeSciBench Gold uses research tasks contributed by practicing life scientists. We excluded six
problems flagged for possible issues with the tasks or evaluation environment and compared both
models on the same remaining 94 problems, with up to eight scored responses per problem at
each reasoning budget. At the highest reasoning budget tested, average scores were 61.3% for
GPT-Rosalind-6 and 44.0% for GPT-Rosalind-5.5.

4 Preparedness

The Preparedness Framework is OpenAI’s approach to tracking and preparing for frontier
capabilities that create new risks of severe harm. Under our framework, we work to track and
mitigate the risk of severe harm, including by implementing safeguards that sufficiently minimize
the risk for highly capable models.

4.1 Preparedness Evaluations

We repeated biological capability evaluations on GPT-Rosalind-6 and use the parent model’s
helpful-only evaluations as additional evidence, given it uses the same weights as GPT-6 Astra.
Helpful-only models are evaluated to elicit capabilities that safety refusals may otherwise conceal.

4.1.1 Biological and Chemical

As we did for GPT-6 Astra, we are treating this launch as High capability in the Biological and
Chemical domain. Below, we describe the evaluations we used to reach this conclusion. We
use the four High capability evaluations and four Critical capability evaluations described in
the GPT-6 Astra system card. The hard-negative protein binding and DNA sequence design
evaluations reported for GPT-Rosalind-5.5 have been retired.

4.1.1.1 ProtocolQA Open-Ended

To evaluate models’ ability to troubleshoot commonly published lab protocols, we modify 108
multiple-choice questions from FutureHouse’s ProtocolQA dataset [3] to be open-ended short
answer questions, which makes the evaluation harder and more realistic than the multiple-choice
version. The questions introduce egregious errors in common published protocols, describe the
wet lab result of carrying out this protocol, and ask how to fix the procedure.

4.1.1.2 TroubleshootingBench

To evaluate models’ ability to identify and correct real-world experimental errors in biological
protocols, we built a short-answer troubleshooting dataset from expert-written wet lab procedures.
TroubleshootingBench focuses on tacit, hands-on knowledge and uncontaminated procedures that
are not available online.

Scientists with a PhD in a relevant biological discipline were asked to transcribe biological protocols
they have personally used in the lab. From these protocols, they created troubleshooting questions,
introducing subtle or realistic execution errors and describing the resulting failed outcome.
Compared to ProtocolQA Open-Ended, which focuses on well-known published procedures,
TroubleshootingBench tests performance on non-public protocols and errors that rely on tacit
procedural knowledge.

4.2 Safeguards

Compared to GPT-6 Astra, GPT-Rosalind-6 features additional deployment controls for its
use in advanced life sciences research. Access is limited to approved customers through our
trusted-access deployment structure where organizations must demonstrate they are conducting
legitimate scientific research with public benefit, have strong governance and safety oversight,
and control access with enterprise-grade security.

Our safeguard approach for GPT-Rosalind-6 has four main components: trusted access controls
for verified higher-capability life sciences use, model-level boundaries against harmful biological
and cyber assistance, monitoring and enforcement mechanisms designed to detect abuse, and
additional contractual requirements to enhance security.

GPT-Rosalind-6 retains GPT-6 Astra’s standard cyber safeguards, including real-time blocking.

4.2.1 Trust-based access

GPT-Rosalind-6 is available only to approved customers. Before granting access, OpenAI reviews
whether the applicant has a legitimate life sciences or related mission, a credible use case,
appropriate research and operational governance, and the ability to safely control access to the
model.

GPT-Rosalind-6 uses the access requirements already applied to GPT-Rosalind-5.5, including the
policy updates introduced since its launch. This release does not broaden eligibility beyond prior
announcements, such as the Rosalind Academic Plan.

For non-government applicants, OpenAI also employs business verification and compliance-
screening checks. These checks help confirm applicant authenticity and reduce the risk of access
by fraudulent, insufficiently attributable, or otherwise unsuitable actors. These checks supplement
OpenAI’s broader review of the applicant’s proposed use case, safety posture, and access scope.

For government applicants, OpenAI maintains a trusted-access pathway for select government
entities working on approved biosecurity, biosafety, public-health preparedness, and biodefense
use cases.

Approved enterprise customers must limit GPT-Rosalind-6 access to authorized users with a
legitimate need and apply least-privilege access controls, appropriate onboarding, and insider
risk controls to their users. Customers must apply qualified human review before model outputs
inform experiments, publications, or consequential decisions.

4.2.2 Model behavior and system safeguards

These evaluations were deliberately created to be difficult. They were built around cases in which
our existing models were not yet giving ideal responses, and this is reflected in the scores below.
We continue monitoring these categories after launch to evaluate online performance and further
adjust safeguards as appropriate.

4.2.2.1 Biological and Chemical safeguards

GPT-Rosalind-6 is designed to be more useful for legitimate advanced biological research than
generally available ChatGPT models. This supports advanced life sciences workflows, including
some dual-use topics, that GPT-6 Astra may refuse in General Access. We still tune the model to
refuse or safely respond to requests that would continue to meaningfully enable severe biological
weaponization. This training is done separately from the training of our classifiers and offline
mitigations to decorrelate our safeguards.

To evaluate the quality of these model-level refusals, we track the safety of model responses to
prompts that originate from held-out synthetic data, red-teaming, and production data. These
benchmarks and their grading continue to be updated, meaning results do not directly translate
to past system cards.

Table 10 reports results across our suite of biosecurity benchmarks, accounting for both model
behavior and safeguards. We note that because GPT-Rosalind-6 is a trusted-access deployment,
we mainly considered the results from the Biosecurity - Severe benchmark. Biosecurity - Benign
evaluates model compliance (being fully helpful without refusals or caveats) on a set of benign
biological and operational questions. The remaining benchmark scores show the fraction of model
responses that pass the configured safety grader on prompts related to biological weaponization.
A response that does not pass was flagged under this grading criterion; this does not necessarily

4.2.2.2 Cyber boundaries

GPT-Rosalind-6 inherits the model and system level cyber safeguards of the GPT-6 Astra base
model and additionally applies a more conservative cyber refusal boundary. The model is intended
to continue to refuse requests that clearly enable unauthorized, destructive, or harmful cyber
activity, including areas such as malware deployment, credential theft, exfiltration, or other
malicious activity.

4.2.3 Enforcement

OpenAI can restrict or revoke access for misuse. OpenAI maintains monitoring, review, and
enforcement processes to help identify potential misuse after access is granted. These processes
are designed to detect activity that may indicate malicious biological, chemical or other activity
that is in violation of OpenAI’s Usage Policies or unauthorized access.

OpenAI uses a variety of signals to assess potential misuse, including automated safety classifiers,
account- or organization-level indicators, customer-provided information, and, where available,
specialist review of potentially high-risk activity. The specific review process depends on the
deployment configuration and the information available to OpenAI.

Before launch, we tested our shared monitoring and enforcement process through a biosecurity
red-teaming effort on GPT-Rosalind-5.5 and are in the process of repeating this for GPT-Rosalind-

6 now that it is deployed. We are not publicly disclosing these details because they contain
specific information about our safeguards, but we shared them with our Safety Advisory Group
committee and other relevant governance bodies.

4.2.4 Security and Access Controls

Approved GPT-Rosalind-6 customers must maintain security controls appropriate for access to
a higher-capability life sciences model. These include strong identity and access management,
administrator controls, least-privilege access controls appropriate to the approved deployment,
and processes to remove access when a user no longer has a legitimate need.

Model access and data-retention arrangements are approved separately. Customers must meet
the security and oversight requirements for their approved deployment.

As with other OpenAI deployments, we expect to continue learning from real-world use. We
will monitor safety signals, customer experience, and emerging misuse patterns, and may update
GPT-Rosalind-6 safeguards as the deployment evolves.
