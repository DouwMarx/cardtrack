Muse Spark Open Evaluation Methodology
We evaluate Muse Spark Open on general agentic, coding, multimodal, knowledge & reasoning,
and safety benchmarks.

Overall Methodology
   ●​ Models and configurations. We compare Muse Spark Open to a global set of leading
      open weight models across a range of sizes, using the highest available reasoning effort
      for each model. For models where we report internal evaluation runs, we use the
      recommended inference parameters from each model provider:
           ○​ Muse Spark Open: reasoning effort = xhigh (512), temperature=1.0, top_p=1.0
           ○​ Inkling: reasoning effort = xhigh (effort=0.99), temperature=1.0, top_p=1.0
           ○​ Beam: Self reported from launch post.
           ○​ Mistral Large 4: Self reported from launch post.
           ○​ Nemotron 3 Ultra: thinking enabled, temperature=1.0, top_p=0.95
           ○​ Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B: reasoning effort = xhigh, temperature=1.0, top_p=0.95
           ○​ GLM 5.3: reasoning effort = max, temperature=1.0, top_p=0.95
           ○​ Kimi K3: reasoning effort = max, temperature=1.0, top_p=0.95 for single-step
               tasks, top_p=1.0 for multi-step agentic tasks
   ●​ Evaluation and score selection: We preserve each benchmark’s tasks, inputs,
      grading, and primary metric as closely as possible. When results are available for all
      models from an official leaderboard or benchmark provider, we obtain results from those
      official sources. Otherwise, we report the highest comparable primary-metric value
      available from our internal evaluation runs, or the model provider’s self-reported results.
      Since weights for Beam and Mistral Large 4 were not broadly available at the time of our
      evaluation, we cite their self-reported numbers and results from Artificial Analysis when
      available. The per-benchmark details in the section below specify the sources of each
      result.
   ●​ Third-party models: Results represent our best-effort evaluations of third-party models,
      obtained using common settings where practical. We note that our evaluation setup (e.g.
      agent tools and system prompts) are not specifically tuned for third-party models, and
      may not reflect their best provider-optimized performance.

Per-benchmark details
General
  ●​ Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.3 consists
     of 10 evaluations: AA-Briefcase, GDPval-AA v2, AutomationBench-AA, Terminal-Bench
     v4.0, SciCode, Humanity's Last Exam, GDP.pdf, CritPt, AA-Omniscience, AA-LCR v1.1.
     We sourced the AA Intelligence Index score for all models (with the exception of Beam,
     which was not available at the time of writing) from Artificial Analysis on October 7, 2026,
     following their documented Intelligence Index benchmarking methodology.​

Agentic
  ●​ JobBench: JobBench is a benchmark of 65 professional tasks across 35 white-collar
     occupations. Tasks were designed to better align with areas that were identified as
     high-priority for agentic delegation by experts in their respective fields. Results for all
     models besides Beam and Mistral Large 4 are sourced from the official leaderboard.
     Since Beam and Mistral Large 4 numbers were not available in their model cards or on
     the JobBench leaderboard, we did not report them.
  ●​ GDPval-AA v2: GDPval-AA v2 assesses models on economically valuable knowledge
     work spanning 44 occupations across key sectors contributing to US GDP. The
     benchmark contains 220 tasks developed by OpenAI in collaboration with industry
     professionals to reflect real-world complexity. Each task requires the model to produce
     one or more deliverable files, run as an agent in AA's open-source Stirrup harness with
     an E2B code-execution sandbox, a 250-turn budget, and access to a range of available
     tools. Results for all models (with the exception of Beam, which was not available at the
     time of writing) are sourced from Artificial Analysis following the documented
     benchmarking methodology on October 7, 2026.
  ●​ MCP-Atlas: MCP Atlas evaluates agents on 1,000 human-authored tasks across 36 real
     Model Context Protocol servers and 220 tools. The public and private splits each contain
     500 tasks. Tasks run against containerized MCP servers with a controlled set of target
     and distractor tools. An LLM judge assigns each ground-truth claim a score: 1 if fulfilled,
     0.5 if partially fulfilled, and 0 if not fulfilled. A task's coverage is the mean of its claim
     scores, and the task passes when coverage is at least 0.75. The primary metric is pass
     rate, the percentage of tasks that pass. We cite Beam’s self-reported number, and use
     results produced by Scale AI for the remaining models following the official leaderboard’s

   agent harness and scoring pipeline. Since Mistral Large 4’s performance was not

●​ OSWorld-Verified: OSWorld-Verified is an agentic computer-use benchmark in which
   the agent operates a full Ubuntu desktop VM. We evaluate on the 361-task split that
   excludes Google Drive–dependent tasks. The agent is given a GUI computer-control tool
   only (no direct shell access) and interacts by taking screenshots and emitting GUI
   actions (click, type, scroll, key press), and may batch several actions per step. Grading is
   execution-based: each task defines programmatic getters/checkers that inspect the final
   VM state and return a 0–1 reward. The VM renders at 1920 × 1080 in all runs, but
   because models are trained against different coordinate conventions, we run each in its
   native format rather than imposing one: our models receive the 1920 × 1080 screenshot
   and emit normalized 0–1000 coordinates, while Inkling receives the same screenshot
   upscaled to 2048 × 1152 and emits absolute pixel coordinates, which the harness maps
   back to the native resolution. Each episode is capped at 200 steps, with screenshot
   truncation enabled. We report the mean per-task reward (sum of all task rewards ÷
   number of tasks), matching the official OSWorld metric, averaged over four attempts per
   task to reduce variance. Results for our model and Inkling are from our own runs; Kimi
   K3's number is self-reported. We do not report Nemotron 3 Ultra, GLM 5.3,
   Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B, or Beam, as they do not support pure-vision GUI inputs under
   these settings. We do not report results for Mistral Large 4 as the model was not
   available for internal testing at the time of evaluation.
●​ Toolathlon-Verified: Toolathlon is an agentic tool-use benchmark in which a language
   agent completes 108 realistic, long-horizon tasks by calling real software tools spanning
   ∼32 applications and ∼600 tools, exposed via MCP servers such as GitHub, Notion,
   filesystem, email, and spreadsheets. We evaluate on Toolathlon-Verified, the current
   release, which substantially revises many tasks to improve correctness and evaluation
   reliability. Results for our model were obtained from the official Toolathlon evaluation
   service with our API. Inkling, Nemotron 3 Ultra, and Kimi K3 are sourced from
   Toolathlon’s official leaderboard, while GLM 5.3 and Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B’s numbers are
   from their self reports. Since Beam and Mistral Large 4 numbers were not available in
   their model cards or on the Toolathlon-Verified leaderboard, we did not report them.​

Coding
  ●​ DeepSWE v1.1: DeepSWE is a long-horizon software-engineering benchmark with 113
     tasks across 91 repositories and five languages: TypeScript, Go, Python, JavaScript,
     and Rust. Each task has a handwritten functional verifier and regression checks. We
     report results from the preferred or self-reported harness settings for each model. We
     source results for Muse Spark Open (with Muse Code harness) from Artificial Analysis,
     and from each model’s self report for Beam (harness unspecified), Mistral Large 4
     (harness unspecified), GLM 5.3 (mini-swe-agent), Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B (Claude Code),
     and Kimi K3 (Kimi Code). Since results for Inkling and Nemotron 3 Ultra were not
     available in their model cards or on the Artificial Analysis leaderboard, we do not report
     results for those models.
  ●​ SWE-Atlas-QnA: SWE-Atlas-QnA evaluates deep code comprehension in
     production-grade software systems. The benchmark spans 11 open-source repositories
     across 4 languages (Go, Python, C, TypeScript), covering mail servers, terminal
     emulators, distributed object storage, observability platforms, and secret scanners. Each
     task runs in a dedicated Docker image with the target repository pre-installed, in a
     sandbox with 2GB RAM and 2 CPUs; answers are graded by a rubric judge. Results for
     Muse Spark Open (with Muse Code harness), Kimi K3 (Kimi Code), GLM 5.3 (Open
     Code), and Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B (Claude Code) and their corresponding harnesses are
     sourced from Artificial Analysis following the documented Coding Agents methodology,
     and we report Beam and Mistral Large 4’s self reported numbers. Since results for
     Inkling and Nemotron 3 Ultra were not available in their model cards or on the Artificial
     Analysis leaderboard, we did not report them.
  ●​ Terminal-Bench 2.1: Terminal-Bench 2.1 evaluates an agent on real-world tasks in a
     terminal environment. It contains 89 curated tasks across software engineering, system
     administration, data processing, model training, and security. For all models besides
     Beam and Mistral Large 4, we report results from Artificial Analysis following the
     documented benchmarking methodology, which uses the Terminus 2 agent harness in
     an E2B sandbox environment. For Beam, we report its self-reported performance, which
     does not specify the harness. Since results for Mistral Large 4 were not available in their
     model card or on the Artificial Analysis leaderboard, we do not report it.

Knowledge & Reasoning
  ●​ Humanity's Last Exam (HLE) Diamond (not tools)​
     HLE-Diamond is a refined 1,000-question subset of Humanity's Last Exam, released by

     CAIS. It is split evenly into 500 reasoning questions and 500 knowledge questions, and
     the questions are designed to be answerable closed-book. We source results for all
     models besides Beam and Mistral Large 4 from Scale AI, which maintains the HLE
     Diamond leaderboard, following the documented methodology.
●​   AA Omniscience Index: AA-Omniscience is Artificial Analysis's evaluation of
     cross-domain knowledge reliability, designed to measure factual recall while explicitly
     penalising hallucination. Rather than a plain correct-answer rate, it reports the
     Omniscience Index (OI), computed as 100 × (correct − incorrect) / (correct + partially
     correct + incorrect + abstained), so a model scores above 0 only when it answers
     correctly more often than it answers incorrectly, and abstention is rewarded over a
     confident wrong answer. Scores range from −100 to +100. We source the AA
     Omniscience Index score for all models besides Beam from Artificial Analysis, and report
     Beam’s self-reported number, which is based on the public split of the Omniscience
     dataset.
●​   SciCode: SciCode contains scientist-curated laboratory problems across 16 scientific
     disciplines, developed by domain experts with the reported evaluation using the 288 test
     set subproblems. The benchmark tests Python programming to solve scientific
     computing tasks: models must generate code that passes every unit test for a
     sub-problem to be credited. Results for all models besides Beam are sourced from
     Artificial Analysis following the documented benchmarking methodology, and we report
     Beam’s self-reported number.
●​   Humanity's Last Exam (HLE): This benchmark consists of highly advanced,
     crowd-sourced questions spanning dozens of subjects that are specifically designed to
     be difficult even for human experts to answer. The questions test advanced scientific
     knowledge, complex abstract reasoning, and niche academic concepts. For the “no
     tools” setting, results for all models besides Beam are sourced from Artificial Analysis
     following the documented benchmarking methodology, which uses the text-only subset
     of 2,158 questions and with no tool use permitted. Results for Beam were sourced from
     their self-report. For the “with tools” setting, we report results from internal runs for our
     model, allowing bash and browser tools with blocklist on huggingface.co, kaggle.com,
     paperswithcode.com, lmsys.org. For all third-party models, we directly cite their
     self-reported “with tools” scores.
●​   GPQA Diamond: The 198-question subset of GPQA contains high-quality,
     multiple-choice questions in biology, physics, and chemistry that were validated by
     domain experts to ensure they cannot be easily looked up on Google. The problems
     require deep, graduate-level scientific reasoning and rigorous technical understanding to
     solve. We cite Beam’s self-reported number, and source results for the remaining
     models from Artificial Analysis following the documented benchmarking methodology.

​Multimodal
Since Nemotron 3 Ultra, GLM 5.3, Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B, and Beam do not natively support
multimodal inputs, we do not run multimodal evals on those models. We also do not report
results for Mistral Large 4, as the model was not available for internal testing at the time of
evaluation. We report BabyVision, SimpleVQA, ZeroBench, and WorldVQA with tool use
enabled.

   ●​ BabyVision: BabyVision contains 388 visual-reasoning questions spanning fine-grained
      discrimination, spatial perception, visual tracking, and visual pattern recognition, all of
      which are included in the reported score. The set contains 135 multiple-choice and 253
      free-form questions. GPT-OSS-120B with high reasoning assigns a binary grade based
      on semantic equivalence to the reference answer. The primary metric is mean accuracy
      across all prompts. For internal runs on Muse Spark and Inkling, each eval was run 5
      times to reduce variance, and we report the average of those runs. We cite Kimi K3’s
      self reported number from their model card.
   ●​ SimpleVQA: SimpleVQA contains bilingual visual-factuality questions in English and
      Chinese. We use a 2,024-question evaluation set: 1,013 English questions and 1,011
      Chinese questions. GPT-OSS-120B with high reasoning grades correctness against the
      reference answer. The primary metric is raw accuracy across the 2,024-question
      evaluation set. We report results from internal runs for all 3 multimodal models and
      report the average of 5 runs.
   ●​ ZeroBench: ZeroBench contains 100 deliberately difficult multimodal-reasoning
      questions. Responses are generated using stochastic sampling. GPT-OSS-120B with
      high reasoning and container access compares the final response with the reference
      answer and assigns a binary semantic-correctness grade. The primary metric is the
      percentage of questions for which at least one evaluated response is judged correct over
      five samples, consistent with the leaderboard. We report internal runs for Muse Spark
      and Inkling, and cite Kimi K3’s self reported number from their model card.
   ●​ WorldVQA: WorldVQA contains 3,500 image-question pairs across nine categories. We
      use the benchmark’s official headline subset: 3,000 questions across eight categories,
      which excludes 500 questions in the Notable People category. GPT-OSS-120B with high
      reasoning grades correctness against the reference answer. The primary metric is mean
      question-level accuracy across the 3,000-question subset. We report results from
      internal runs for our model and Inkling, and cite Kimi K3’s self reported number from their
      model card.

  ●​ IntPhys 2: IntPhys 2 is a video benchmark that tests whether a model recognizes
     physically impossible events in complex synthetic environments. It contains rich scenes
     and backgrounds, and comprises 1,416 videos organised as matched quadruplets —
     two physically possible and two impossible variants of each scene, where a small
     change makes an otherwise plausible event impossible. The model is asked to judge the
     plausibility of each video, and we report accuracy using the benchmark's official
     violation-of-expectation protocol on the main split with 1012 videos. Results for Muse
     Spark Open, Inkling, and Kimi K3 are from internal runs.
  ●​ Video-MME v2: Video-MME v2 is a public comprehensive video-understanding
     benchmark containing 800 videos and 3,200 eight-option multiple-choice questions
     spanning three cognitive levels, with word-level timestamped subtitles. We evaluate
     0-shot on the full set and report accuracy under the benchmark's official grouped scoring
     (relevance and logic question groups), following the official evaluation code. We run with
     subtitles in the prompt and no audio. Results for Muse Spark Open, Inkling, and Kimi K3
     are from internal runs.

Safety
  ●​ FORTRESS adversarial: FORTRESS: Frontier Risk Evaluation for National Security
     and Public Safety (Knight et al., 2025) is a benchmark featuring over 1,010
     expert-crafted adversarial prompts designed to evaluate the safeguards of frontier LLMs
     (500 in the public dataset) from Scale AI. FORTRESS assesses model responses
     across three domains: Chemical, Biological, Radiological, Nuclear and Explosive
     (CBRNE); Political Violence & Terrorism; and Criminal & Financial Illicit Activities. The
     primary metrics are Average Risk Score (ARS) on the adversarial half – the mean
     fraction of rubric criteria a response satisfies (lower is better), and the Over-Refusal
     Score (ORS) – the percentage of benign prompts that a model refuses to answer (lower
     is better). Results for all models besides Beam and Mistral Large 4 are sourced from
     Scale’s leaderboard, which reports on the private split of FORTRESS. Since results for
     Beam and Mistral Large 4 were not available in their model card or on the leaderboard,
     we do not report on these models.
  ●​ StrongREJECTv2: StrongRejectv2 is an internal benchmark of 202 disallowed
     behaviors, adapted from StrongREJECT (Souly et al., 2024) with internally curated
     behaviors, to evaluate robustness against general disallowed requests on text-only
     inputs. Each behavior is attacked under five conditions: basic and advanced jailbreak
     tactic libraries, basic and advanced adversarial developer prompts, and an adaptive
     multi-turn attacker. A behavior counts as broken if any attempt yields a response that
     passes the StrongREJECT autograder. We report the mean of two attack success rates
